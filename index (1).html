Meta Quest 3 in Your Classroom

A step-by-step guide for elementary school teachers

Page 1

Meet the Meta Quest 3

The Meta Quest 3 is a headset you wear on your face. It has two small screens inside that show a digital world all around you. You can look in any direction by turning your head. You use two hand controllers to interact with what you see.

The headset does not need a computer or phone. Everything runs on the device itself.

Parts of the Headset

The Headset

The main device. You wear it over your eyes like a large pair of goggles.

The Head Strap

Holds the headset on your head. You can tighten or loosen it for comfort.

The Lenses

Two lenses inside the headset. You look through them to see the virtual world.

The Power Button

Located on the right side of the headset. Press it to turn the device on or off.

The Volume Buttons

Located on the bottom edge of the headset. Press them to raise or lower the sound.

The Charging Port

Located on the left side. Plug in the included cable here to charge the headset.

The Controllers

Two handheld devices, one for each hand. You use them to point, select, and interact.

Turning the Headset On

  1. Press and hold the power button on the right side of the headset.
  2. Wait a few seconds. The screen inside will light up.
  3. Put the headset on and adjust the straps until it feels comfortable.

Turning the Headset Off

  1. Press and hold the power button until a menu appears on the screen inside the headset.
  2. Point your controller at "Power Off" and press the trigger to shut it down.
Tip

Power the headset off completely at the end of each day. This saves the battery for your next session.

Adjusting the Fit

A good fit is important. The lenses should line up directly with your eyes for a clear picture.

  • Loosen the head strap before putting the headset on.
  • Place the headset over your eyes and tighten the strap until it is snug but not tight.
  • Slide the lenses left or right to match the natural spacing of your eyes. Use the small slider on the bottom of the headset.
  • If the image looks blurry, try adjusting the lens position until it clears up.

Charging the Headset

  • Use the included USB-C cable to charge the headset.
  • A full charge takes about 2 to 3 hours.
  • A fully charged headset lasts about 2 to 3 hours of active use.
  • Charge the headset the night before you plan to use it in class.
Classroom Note

If you have more than one headset, label each headset and its cable with a number or name. This makes storing and charging much easier.

Types of VR Experiences

Not all virtual reality experiences work the same way. There are three main types you will encounter as a teacher. Each one offers a different kind of experience for your students.

Head-Mounted Display (HMD)

You wear the headset on your head. The screens inside show a 3D world all around you.

You turn your head to look in any direction and the view changes to match.

You use the hand controllers to interact with objects in the world.

Example: A student explores a virtual coral reef and uses the controller to examine sea animals up close.

Desktop VR

You view a virtual experience on a regular computer or laptop screen.

You use a mouse or keyboard to look around and move through the experience.

You are watching the experience from outside rather than being inside it.

Example: A teacher previews a virtual field trip on their laptop before showing it to students.

360 Video

A 360 video is filmed with a special camera that captures all directions at once.

You can look left, right, up, and down inside the video.

You cannot interact with anything in the video. You are a viewer, not a participant.

Example: Students watch a 360 video of a rainforest and look around to see trees, animals, and the sky.

Which Type Is Best for Your Classroom?

  • Use a head-mounted display when you want students to feel like they are inside the experience. It works well for science, social studies, and creative projects.
  • Use desktop VR when you want to show the whole class something on a screen, or when a student cannot wear the headset.
  • Use 360 videos when you want students to explore a real place or event. Many free 360 videos are available on YouTube.
Classroom Note

The Meta Quest 3 supports all three types. Students can wear it as an HMD, you can cast the view to a TV or computer screen for the whole class to watch, or students can watch 360 videos through the browser or apps.

Quick Comparison

Type Device Needed Can You Interact? Best For
Head-Mounted Display The headset Yes Hands-on, interactive lessons
Desktop VR A computer Limited Previewing content or whole-class viewing
360 Video Headset or computer No Virtual field trips and real-world exploration

The Controllers

The Meta Quest 3 comes with two controllers, one for each hand. You use them to point at things on screen, press buttons, and interact with the virtual world.

You do not need to press hard. Light, deliberate movements are enough.

How to Point and Select

Pointing and selecting is how you navigate all menus and apps.

  1. Hold a controller at a natural angle, as if you are pointing at something across the room.
  2. A thin beam of light will appear from the front of the controller. This is your pointer.
  3. Move the controller until the beam rests on the button or item you want.
  4. Press the trigger with your index finger to select it.

Controller Tips

  • The controllers use AA batteries. Check the battery level in the headset settings before class.
  • Keep a set of spare batteries in your classroom.
  • If a controller stops responding, remove the battery and reinsert it.
  • Each controller has a wrist strap. Make sure students wear the strap during use to prevent drops.
Tip

Practice using the controllers yourself before your first lesson. Spend 10 minutes pointing, selecting, and pressing buttons. This will make it easier to guide your students.

Checking the Internet Connection

The Meta Quest 3 needs a Wi-Fi connection to use apps and the browser. Without a connection, many features will not work.

Check the connection before class starts so you are not troubleshooting in front of students.

How to Check Your Connection

  1. Put on the headset and pick up the right controller.
  2. Press the Meta button (the small round button below the thumbstick on the right controller).
  3. The main menu bar will appear at the bottom of your view.
  4. Point your controller at the signal bars or clock area on the right side of the menu bar and press the trigger.
  5. A small panel will open. It will say "Connected" and show the network name if you are online.

How to Connect to a Wi-Fi Network

  1. Press the Meta button to open the main menu.
  2. Point at "Settings" in the menu bar and press the trigger.
  3. Select "Wi-Fi" from the list on the left side of the screen.
  4. The headset will show a list of nearby networks.
  5. Point at your school network and press the trigger to select it.
  6. An on-screen keyboard will appear. Type in the network password.
  7. Point at "Connect" and press the trigger.
School Networks

Some school networks show a login page after you connect. If this happens, open the browser and visit any website. The login page will appear automatically. Enter your school login and then proceed.

What to Do If You Cannot Connect

  • Make sure Wi-Fi is turned on. Go to Settings, then Wi-Fi, and check that the toggle is set to on.
  • Move the headset closer to the Wi-Fi router.
  • Restart the headset. Press and hold the power button and select "Restart."
  • Contact your school technology coordinator if the problem continues. Some school networks block new devices by default and need to approve the headset before it can connect.
Before Your Lesson

Connect the headset to Wi-Fi the day before your lesson. This gives you time to fix any problems without students waiting.

Using the On-Screen Keyboard

When you need to type a password or web address, a keyboard appears on screen inside the headset.

  • Point your controller at a letter and press the trigger to type it.
  • Point at the backspace key to delete a character.
  • Point at the check mark or "Enter" key when you are done.

Using the Browser

The Meta Quest 3 has a built-in web browser. You can use it to visit websites while wearing the headset. The browser works like a regular browser on a computer.

You need a Wi-Fi connection to use the browser. See Page 4 if you have not connected yet.

How to Open the Browser

  1. Press the Meta button on the right controller to open the main menu.
  2. Point at "Apps" in the menu bar and press the trigger.
  3. Your app library will open. Look for the app called "Browser."
  4. Point at the Browser app and press the trigger to open it.
  5. A browser window will appear floating in front of you inside the headset.
Shortcut

To add the browser to the bottom menu bar for quick access, open the app library, point at Browser, hold the trigger for one second, and select "Pin to Taskbar."

How to Visit a Website

  1. Point your controller at the address bar at the top of the browser window.
  2. Press the trigger. The bar will highlight and an on-screen keyboard will appear.
  3. Type the web address using the on-screen keyboard.
  4. Press the Enter key on the keyboard. The website will load.

Navigating Inside the Browser

  • Point your controller at a link or button and press the trigger to click it.
  • To scroll a page, point at the page and move the thumbstick up or down.
  • Press the back arrow on the left side of the browser bar to return to the previous page.
  • Press the circular arrow button to reload a page.

Resizing and Moving the Browser Window

  • To move the window, point at the top bar of the browser, hold the grip button, and move the controller to drag it to a new position.
  • To resize the window, point at a corner or edge of the browser window, hold the grip button, and move the controller.

Opening a Second Browser Window

You can have two browser windows open at the same time and place them side by side.

  1. With the browser open, look for the small plus button at the top of the window.
  2. Point at the plus button and press the trigger to open a new window.
  3. Both windows will appear. You can reposition each one independently.

Classroom Tips for the Browser

  • Bookmark websites before class so students can find them quickly without typing.
  • Test all websites on your school network before the lesson. Some sites may be blocked by the school's content filter.
  • To watch a 360 video on YouTube, open YouTube in the browser, search for the video, and select it. The video will play in 360 mode automatically when you are wearing the headset.
  • If a website does not load correctly, press the reload button or go to the browser settings to clear the history.
Classroom Note

You can cast the headset display to a TV or computer screen so the whole class sees what one student is viewing. Ask your technology coordinator about setting up casting in your classroom.
